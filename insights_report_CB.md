**Title:** Coherent Breathing Meditation Modality Comparison

**Subtitle:** An analysis comparing the neurocognitive and affective responses to 'Audio' and 'Audio & Video' coherent breathing guided meditations.

**Date:** July 12, 2026

---

## Summary

> This experiment aimed to determine the optimal modality for coherent breathing guided meditations, comparing an 'Audio' only version with an 'Audio & Video' version. EEG data was collected and processed using machine learning algorithms to characterize neural responses across various cognitive and affective dimensions, known as Performance Metrics (PMs). These PM values were then baseline-corrected to assess the change in each metric during the meditation compared to a pre-meditation baseline. The overall findings suggest distinct patterns in stress reduction, engagement, and interest between the two modalities, which can inform future meditation design.

---

## Insight 1 — Enhanced Stress Reduction with Audiovisual Guidance

> The 'Audio & Video' meditation modality shows a more pronounced decrease in stress levels compared to the 'Audio' only version.  
>   
> The 'Audio & Video' modality led to a substantial reduction in Stress, with an average baseline-corrected value of −26.99. In contrast, the 'Audio' only modality showed a slight increase in Stress, averaging \+0.52. This suggests that incorporating visual elements alongside audio guidance can be particularly effective in alleviating feelings of tension during coherent breathing exercises, promoting a deeper state of relaxation.

## Insight 2 — Superior Engagement Through Audiovisual Experience

> Participants experienced higher levels of engagement when guided by both audio and visual cues during the meditation.  
>   
> The 'Audio & Video' modality fostered a notably higher level of Engagement, with an average baseline-corrected increase of 12.85. The 'Audio' only modality also showed an increase in Engagement, averaging 4.78. This indicates that the combination of visual and auditory stimuli is more effective in immersing individuals in the meditation activity, potentially minimizing distractions and enhancing focus.

## Insight 3 — Varying Impact on Interest Across Modalities

> Both meditation modalities were associated with a decrease in interest, with the 'Audio & Video' version showing a more pronounced trend towards lower interest.  
>   
> While both modalities exhibited a decrease in Interest from baseline, the 'Audio & Video' version showed a larger drop, averaging −14.85, compared to the 'Audio' version's average of −1.77. This suggests that while 'Audio & Video' promotes higher engagement and stress reduction, the specific content or pacing might be leading to a trend of aversion for some individuals. Further investigation into the visual content's design could be beneficial.

## Insight 4 — Consistent Attention Levels with Both Modalities

> Both 'Audio' and 'Audio & Video' meditations show comparable trends in maintaining or slightly increasing attention.  
>   
> Both modalities appear to support focused attention during the meditation, with 'Audio' showing an average baseline-corrected Attention of 1.80 and 'Audio & Video' showing 2.25. While 'Audio & Video' has a slightly higher average, the levels are generally similar. This indicates that regardless of the presence of visual cues, the core coherent breathing practice itself is effective in promoting a state of focused concentration.

## Insight 5 — Greater Calmness with Audiovisual Guided Breathing

> The 'Audio & Video' modality led to a more pronounced decrease in excitement, suggesting a deeper state of calmness or relaxation.  
>   
> Excitement levels decreased in both conditions, which can be indicative of a calming or relaxing experience. The 'Audio & Video' modality showed a larger average decrease in Excitement at −19.39, compared to 'Audio' at −5.58. This suggests that the combined sensory input of 'Audio & Video' may facilitate a more profound state of tranquility and reduced arousal.

---

## Recommendation 1 — Leverage Audiovisual for Deep Relaxation and Immersion

> The 'Audio & Video' modality consistently shows superior performance in reducing Stress and increasing Engagement. When the primary goal of your breathwork is to achieve a profound state of relaxation and minimize feelings of tension, integrating visual guidance appears to be the more effective approach. Consider this modality for users seeking a more encompassing and focused meditation experience.

## Recommendation 2 — Optimize Audiovisual Content to Enhance Interest

> Despite its strengths in stress reduction and engagement, 'Audio & Video' trended towards lower Interest compared to 'Audio'. This could indicate that certain visual or auditory aspects of the current 'Audio & Video' content might be less appealing or even slightly aversive for some users. Experiment with different visual themes, colors, patterns, or accompanying sounds to ensure the experience remains positively engaging and attractive throughout the session.

## Recommendation 3 — Utilize Audio-Only for Flexible or Low-Distraction Environments

> While 'Audio & Video' offers strong benefits, 'Audio' still provides a positive impact on Engagement and Attention, and a modest reduction in Excitement. It may be ideal for situations where a user cannot watch a screen, prefers to meditate with eyes closed, or in environments where visual stimuli could be distracting. Offer 'Audio' as a flexible alternative, ensuring that the core breathing guidance remains clear and soothing.

## Method summary

| Parameter | Value |
| :---- | :---- |
| Subject | N \= 1 (author is subject and experimenter) |
| Health screening | No neurological conditions; no current psychiatric medication |
| EEG device | Emotiv EPOC X, 14 channels, saline (wet) sensors |
| Metrics source | Emotiv Studio Performance Metrics: Stress, Attention, Engagement, Excitement, Interest |
| Repeats | 3 full sessions |
| Design | Within-subject; each session included both conditions |
| Condition order | Fixed every session — Audio & Video first, Audio second. Not randomized or counterbalanced. |
| Eyes | Open throughout the entire session, all phases, both conditions — no eyes-closed condition recorded |
| Baseline | 240 seconds, seated, eyes open, resting |
| Each guided block | 5 minutes (300 seconds) |
| Breathing pattern | Coherent breathing: 6 s inhale / 6 s exhale (10 breaths/min, 0.1 Hz), seated upright |
| Audio asset | Created in Logic Pro; auditory pacing cues for the 6 s/6 s cycle |
| Visual asset | Created in TouchDesigner; synchronized visual pacing animation for the same cycle |
| Pipeline | Emotiv Studio used to configure the experiment and compute Performance Metrics |
